Random walks and node2vec

If you wander a network at random, do you explore far or stay close to home, and can you choose?

What it’s forSamples of a network, as sentences

A random walk starts somewhere and keeps taking a random edge. Many short walks are a cheap sample of a large network. Places that often turn up in the same walks are related.

Reach for biased walks when you want to learn a vector for each vertex, as node2vec does, to feed a recommender or a classifier. Two dials, p and q, choose what “related” means. It can mean being in the same neighbourhood, or playing the same role.

Compared withOther ways to wander a network

A node2vec walk costs little per step and can lean toward a breadth-first or a depth-first view. One walk says little on its own, so you need many.

Example: a park of fifteen places. There are woods round Ash, a river path, and a beach on the far side. A walker takes thirty random steps from Ash. Two dials, p and q, bias each step.

The ideaA walk that remembers one step

A plain random walk takes each path out of the current place with equal chance, and forgets where it came from. Grover and Leskovec’s node2vec remembers one step back. Every path out of the current place leads to a place 0, 1 or 2 steps from the previous place. Each path gets a weight to match. Going straight back is 1/p. Going to a place next to the previous one is 1. Going anywhere further is 1/q. With p = q = 1 it is the plain walk again.

A small q pushes the walker onward. Its walks resemble a depth-first search and reach across the network, here from the woods to the beach. A large q keeps it circling one neighbourhood, like a breadth-first search, so its walks describe the local structure.

node2vec feeds many such walks to a word-embedding model, as if they were sentences. Places that turn up near each other in walks get similar vectors. So choosing q chooses what “similar” means. It can mean the same neighbourhood, or the same role, such as being a bridge.

randomWalk(from:length:returnParameter:inOutParameter:using:) takes the generator as an argument. A seeded one gives the same walk on every platform, which is how the twenty-walk summary is repeatable.

Where it breaksOne walk proves little

Thirty steps is a small sample. Two seeds with the same p and q can give very different walks, which is why the summary averages twenty. Embeddings use hundreds of walks per vertex.

The bias is also only one step deep. It can’t see that the beach is far away, only whether the next place is next to the last. And on a graph with hubs, any walk keeps passing through them. That is the idea behind PageRank, but a nuisance when hubs aren’t what you want to learn about.
