Random walks and node2vec

If you wander a network at random, do you explore far or stay close to home, and can you choose?

Example: a park of fifteen places: woods round Ash, a river path, and a beach on the far side. A walker takes thirty random steps from Ash. Two dials, p and q, bias each step.

The ideaA walk that remembers one step

  
A plain random walk takes each path out of the current place with equal chance, and forgets where it came from. Grover and Leskovec’s node2vec remembers one step back. Every path out of the current place leads somewhere at distance 0, 1 or 2 from the previous place, and gets a weight to match: 1/p for going straight back, 1 for a place next to the previous one, 1/q for anywhere further. With p = q = 1 it is the plain walk again.

  
A small q pushes the walker onward, so its walks resemble a depth-first search and reach across the network, here from the woods to the beach. A large q keeps it circling one neighbourhood, like a breadth-first search, so its walks describe the local structure. node2vec feeds many such walks, as if they were sentences, to a word-embedding model, and places that turn up near each other in walks get similar vectors. Choosing q chooses what “similar” means: the same neighbourhood, or the same role, such as being a bridge.

  
randomWalk(from:length:returnParameter:inOutParameter:using:) takes the generator explicitly, so a seeded one gives the same walk on every platform, which is how the twenty-walk summary is repeatable.


  
Where it breaksOne walk proves little

  
Thirty steps is a small sample: two seeds with the same p and q can give very different walks, which is why the summary averages twenty. Embeddings use hundreds of walks per vertex. The bias is also only one step deep: it can’t see that the beach is far away, only whether the next place is next to the last. And on a graph with hubs, any walk keeps passing through them, which is the idea behind PageRank but a nuisance when hubs aren’t what you want to learn about.
