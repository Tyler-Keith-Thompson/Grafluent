Paste a graph

Got a graph of your own? Paste it, see it, and search it.

Example: a small town in DOT. Change it, or pick another format for an example in that one, or paste your own: up to 120 vertices, undirected. Edges with a weight attribute that is a whole number use it for Dijkstra; the rest weigh 1.

The ideaRead, draw, run

  
Each format has an initializer on any graph that can be built up: UndirectedAdjacencyList<String>(dot:), (graphML:), (graph6:vertex:) and (edgeList:). They read bytes, so a file’s contents go straight in, and attributes come out through closures as the reader meets them, keyed by the new edge’s position. Nothing about the text is kept once the graph is built.

  
The drawing is a force-directed layout from a fixed seed, so the same text always draws the same way. Breadth-first search and Dijkstra are the lazy, step-by-step forms the BFS and Dijkstra pages use, and the components are coloured as on the components page.


  
Where it breaksErrors, and what readers leave out

  
A reader that can’t make sense of the text throws a ParseError with its kind and the line and column of the offending character, counted in Unicode scalars, so an editor can jump straight there. Errors are typed: a catch gets a ParseError, never an arbitrary error.

  
Formats carry more than a graph: DOT’s colours and shapes, GraphML’s typed keys, an edge list’s extra columns. The readers hand every attribute to the closures and keep none themselves, so anything this page doesn’t ask for, such as positions written in the file, is read and dropped. graph6 has no names at all: its vertices are numbers, here turned into text by the vertex: closure.
