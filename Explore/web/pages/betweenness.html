Betweenness

When news travels the shortest way through an office, who does it keep passing through?

What it’s forFinding the go-betweens

Betweenness scores each person by how often they sit on the shortest route between two others. A high score marks a broker. Take that person out, and many pairs need a longer route, or can’t reach each other at all.

Reach for it to find bottlenecks: the router most traffic crosses, the airport between two regions, the one person who links two teams. The same count for links finds the busiest link.

Compared withOther ways to say who matters

Betweenness is the measure for brokers. It costs one search per person, so on a very large network you estimate it from a sample. In the table, n is the number of people and m the number of links.

Example: nine people in an office, and a line between each pair who talk. There are two teams, and a few people talk to both.

The ideaCount the shortest paths through you

For every pair of people, look at the shortest chains of conversations between them. If there is one, everyone in its middle gets a point. If there are several equally short ones, the point is split between them. With two shortest paths, each counts a half. A person’s betweenness is their total over all pairs.

The same count for links is edge betweenness. Every shortest path gives each of its links a share, including a pair’s own direct link. The link with the most is the one most traffic would cross. Removing such links one at a time is how Girvan–Newman splits a network into communities.

Counting pair by pair, as this page does, repeats a lot of work. Brandes’ algorithm gets every score from one breadth-first search per person. It counts the shortest paths from that person to everyone. Then it walks back from the farthest, and each person passes their share to whoever came before them. That takes O(nm) time, and it is what betweennessCentrality() runs. By default the scores are divided by the number of pairs, so they lie between 0 and 1. With normalized: false you get the raw counts shown here.

Where it breaksShortest isn’t how everything travels

Betweenness assumes things move along shortest paths and nowhere else. Gossip, infections and money often wander. A person on many second-shortest paths scores nothing. If influence spreads more like a random walk, PageRank or current-flow betweenness may fit better.

On a large network, one search per person is too many. With pivots: or pivotCount:, betweennessCentrality searches from a sample of people and scales up. The estimate is good when the sample is random and big enough. To weigh the links, pass weight:. Paths are then shortest by total weight, and two paths tie only when their sums are exactly equal.
