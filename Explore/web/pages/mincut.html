Minimum cut

Say itnah-gah-MOH-chee ee-bah-RAH-kee/naɡamotɕi ibaɾaki/

What’s the fewest cables you’d have to cut to split a network in two?

What it’s forHow fragile a network is

A cut splits a network into two groups. Its value is the total weight of the links between the groups. The minimum cut is the cheapest such split. It tells you the least that a digger, a storm or an attacker has to break to cut someone off.

Reach for it to find the weak spot in a network of cables, roads or pipes. Sometimes you need the cheapest way to keep two particular places apart. Sometimes you want that answer for every pair at once. Each has its own method.

Compared withOther ways to find a cut

For the single weakest spot, use minimumCut(capacity:). It needs no flows at all. For many pairs, build a Gomory–Hu tree once and read every answer from it. In the table, n is the number of offices and m the number of links.

Example: eight offices joined by data links. Each link shows how many cables it has. Cutting a link means cutting all its cables.

The ideaOne tree answers every pair

To keep two offices apart, the fewest cables to cut equals the most data that can flow between them. That is the max-flow min-cut theorem from maximum flow. So one flow answers one pair.

Gomory and Hu showed that n − 1 flows answer every pair. The answers fit in a tree on the same offices. The fewest cables between any two offices is the lightest edge on the tree path between them. The timeline builds that tree one edge at a time, by Gusfield’s method, as gomoryHuTree(capacity:) does. The cheapest cut overall is the tree’s lightest edge.

minimumCut(capacity:) finds that cheapest split without any flows. Nagamochi and Ibaraki’s method puts the offices in order, each one as tightly tied to those before it as possible. Two offices tied by at least the best cut so far can’t be worth separating, so it merges them and goes on.

Where it breaksCheapest isn’t always the split you want

The minimum cut often just cuts off one poorly connected office. That is right for measuring fragility. It is wrong when you want two halves of about the same size with few links between them. That is a different and much harder problem. Community detection is usually what people want there.

Capacities must not be negative. On a directed network, minimumCut(capacity:) counts only the links leaving one side, and it uses Hao and Orlin’s method instead.
