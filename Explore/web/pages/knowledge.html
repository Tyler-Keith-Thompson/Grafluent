Knowledge graphs

A model read a few paragraphs and wrote down fourteen claims, spelling Ada Lovelace three ways. Which of them belong in the prompt for a question about her and the Analytical Engine?

What it’s forFacts as a graph, and the right few back out

A knowledge graph stores facts as triples: a subject, a relation and an object, which are a directed edge with its relation attached. Retrieval that works from such a graph, often called GraphRAG, answers a question by finding the entities it names, taking the facts around them and writing those facts into a prompt. The graph side is four small steps: merge the duplicate entities, rank the entities from the question, cut the neighbourhood to a budget, and write it out.

Reach for it when a question needs facts that sit several hops apart, which a plain search over passages tends to miss, or when the same entity is named in many documents and the answer should join them.

Compared withOther ways to pick facts for a prompt

Ranking from the question’s entities favours facts on the paths between them; a degree cut favours hubs. Community summaries answer broad questions, and passage search answers narrow ones.

Example: fourteen claims about Ada Lovelace, Charles Babbage and their engines, as an extractor wrote them, with Lovelace spelled three ways and Babbage three ways.

Step throughMerge, rank, retrieve, write

The ideaFour calls, one graph

The relations live in an edge map on a directed pseudograph, so two claims about the same pair stay separate edges with their own relation. Merging is a quotient by a key: quotient(of:byKey:) makes one vertex of every spelling that maps to the same canonical name, and the page rebuilds the graph from those names so the relations come along. Ranking is pageRank(personalization:) with the question’s entities as the places a random reader keeps jumping back to, so entities on the paths between them score high and far corners score low. Retrieval is egoGraphView from the question’s entities, two hops out, ignoring the arrows’ direction and keeping the best-ranked entities when the budget runs out. Writing is writeEdgeList with the relation as a column and a delimiter that allows spaces in names: one line per fact, subject, object and relation.

Where it breaksThe key, and the budget

The merge is only as good as the key. A key that maps two different people to one name joins their facts, and one that misses a spelling leaves a duplicate with its own facts; in practice the key comes from an entity-resolution step, and the quotient applies it. The budget is a count of entities, not of facts, so a hub inside the budget brings every relation it has. And personalized PageRank ranks by structure alone: a fact that matters to the question but sits three hops away stays out, which is where community summaries help.
