Graph coloring

What’s the fewest exam slots so that no student has two exams at once?

Example: eight exams, with a line between any two that share a student.

What it’s forSharing out slots without clashes

A colouring gives every vertex a colour so that no line joins two vertices of the same colour. Here a colour is a time slot. Reach for it whenever things that clash need different resources, such as exams and rooms, radio stations and frequencies, or variables and registers in a compiler.

Compared withOther ways to colour

Greedy colouring is fast and usually close. Only the exact search promises the fewest colours, and it can be slow. In the table, n is the number of exams and m the number of clashes.

The ideaA colour per time slot

Give each exam a colour, one colour per time slot. No two exams joined by a line may share a colour. Greedy colouring goes through the exams one by one. Each exam gets the first colour that none of its neighbours has. The true minimum is the chromatic number. Finding it takes a search, and the page lets you compare the two.

Where it breaksA bad order

Greedy’s answer depends on its order. Here, in alphabetical order, it needs four slots. Two would be enough.

The exams fall into two groups. Each exam clashes with every exam in the other group except one partner. Alphabetical order keeps reaching a partner pair together, and it gives each pair a new slot. Picking the most constrained exam next (DSATUR) finds two slots. On a two-sided graph like this one it always does.
