A* search

If you only care about one destination, can you skip the rest of town?

Example: a grid of city blocks with a few walls, walking from Home to a café across town. Click blocks to put up or take down walls.

In SwiftThe city grid, in Grafluent







  
The ideaDijkstra with a sense of direction

  
A* is Dijkstra with a sense of direction. Alongside the distance walked so far, it adds a guess of the distance still to go, here the straight-line distance to the café. Places that point the right way get settled first, so the search reaches the café after exploring a fraction of the grid. Switch between A* and Dijkstra on the grid and watch the explored region: Dijkstra’s is a circle, spreading evenly in every direction until the edges of the grid cut it off; A*’s is a narrow teardrop pointed at the café.

  
The straight line is a good guess because no walk can beat it: walls only make the walk longer. A guess that never overestimates is called admissible, and with one A* still finds the shortest route. It just stops looking at blocks that point away from the café.

  
The guess sits in heuristic:, a closure from a block to a number. Pass one that always returns zero and A* is exactly Dijkstra.


  
Where it breaksA guess that says “far” about somewhere close

  
The guess must never overestimate. A guess that says “far” about a place that is actually close can make A* settle on a longer route. Press Overestimate ×3 and every guess is tripled: A* now rushes straight at the café, hugs the wall, and settles for a walk longer than the shortest. With some walls it also settles a block too early and finds a shorter way to it later; the block is then opened again, a red marble on the timeline. Reopening repairs the blocks it reaches, but once the café itself is settled the search is over.

  
Grafluent doesn’t check the guess, because it can’t know the true distances in advance. Overestimating on purpose is a known trade: fewer blocks settled, a route that may be a little longer.
