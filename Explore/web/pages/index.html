Explore Grafluent

Watch graph algorithms think. Every page runs the real library in your browser, one step at a time.

A graph describes anything made of connected parts. The parts are called vertices. They might be cities, people, web pages or the tasks in a build. The connections are called edges. They might be roads, friendships, links or dependencies between tasks. Once a problem is drawn this way, a few dozen well-studied algorithms answer most of the questions people ask. They find the quickest route, the right order, the weakest point and the groups that hang together.

Each page takes one of those algorithms. It says what the algorithm is for and what else could do the job. Then it lets you watch it work, one step at a time, on a small example you can change. Every page runs the real Grafluent library in your browser, so what you see is what your code would do.

How the pages work

  • What it’s for, and how it compares with other ways to do the job.
  • A small graph you can click and edit.
  • Step, play, go back, or drag the timeline. Each marble on the timeline is one thing the algorithm did.
  • The idea: why it works.
  • Where it breaks: the input that fools it, and what to use instead.
  • In Swift: the exact code the page is running.

Many algorithms are named after the people who found them. Where a name is hard to say, the page shows how to say it.
