Lowest common ancestor

Kate and Leo are cousins of some sort. Who is the most recent ancestor they share, and what kind of cousins does that make them?

Example: five generations of a family, with Rose at the top. Each person is joined to one parent, which keeps it a tree.

What it’s forWhere two branches meet

The lowest common ancestor of two vertices in a rooted tree is where their branches meet. Reach for it to find the distance between two vertices in a tree, the merge base of two branches in version control, or the most recent common ancestor of two species.

The ideaLevel up, then climb together

In a rooted tree, the lowest common ancestor of two vertices is the deepest vertex that is an ancestor of both. A vertex counts as its own ancestor.

To find it, first bring the deeper vertex up to the other’s depth, one parent at a time. Then move both up together while they differ. They meet at the lowest common ancestor. That takes as many steps as the depths. It is what lowestCommonAncestor(of:_:) does on a RootedTree.

The ancestor gives more answers. The number of edges between two vertices is their depths added, less twice the ancestor’s depth. That is distance(from:to:). In a family, count the generations down from the ancestor to each person. One each makes siblings. Two each makes first cousins. The difference between the two counts is how many times “removed”.

Compared withOne question or many

For a handful of questions, climbing is simplest. LowestCommonAncestors(family) does O(n) work once, on the order a depth-first walk visits the tree. After that it answers each question in constant time. Here n is the number of people.

Where it breaksReal families aren’t trees

In a real family everyone has two parents, and distant cousins marry. So the same ancestor can be reached along several lines. That makes a directed acyclic graph. Two people can have several lowest common ancestors, and the climb no longer works. Take ancestors(of:) for each person and intersect them to get all the shared ones.
