Lowest common ancestor

Kate and Leo are cousins of some sort. Who is the most recent ancestor they share, and what kind of cousins does that make them?

Example: five generations of a family, each person joined to their parent, with Rose at the top. (One parent per person keeps it a tree.)

In SwiftThe family above, in Grafluent







  
The ideaLevel up, then climb together

  
In a rooted tree, the lowest common ancestor of two vertices is the deepest vertex that is an ancestor of both, a vertex counting as its own ancestor. To find it, first bring the deeper one up to the other’s depth, one parent at a time. Then, while they differ, move both up together. They meet exactly at the lowest common ancestor. That takes as many steps as the depths, and is what lowestCommonAncestor(of:_:) does on a RootedTree.

  
It answers more than family questions. The number of edges between two vertices is their depths minus twice the ancestor’s depth, which is distance(from:to:). In version control, the merge base of two branches is their lowest common ancestor in the commit history; in biology, it is the most recent common ancestor of two species on a tree of life.

  
Here the two generation counts down from the ancestor name the relationship: one each is siblings, two each is first cousins, and a difference between them is how many times “removed”.


  
Where it breaksMany questions, and real families

  
Climbing costs the depth on every question. For many questions on one tree, LowestCommonAncestors(family) does O(n) work once, on the order a depth-first walk visits the tree, and then answers each question in constant time.

  
A real family isn’t a tree: everyone has two parents, and distant cousins marry, so the same ancestor can be reached along several lines. That is a directed acyclic graph, where two people can have several lowest common ancestors and the climb above no longer applies; ancestors(of:) on each and an intersection gives all the shared ones.
