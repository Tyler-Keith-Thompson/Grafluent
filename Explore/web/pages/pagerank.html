PageRank

Which pages on a web matter most?

What it’s forImportance from links

PageRank scores every page in a network of links. Such a score is called a centrality. A page scores high when pages that score high link to it. The scores add up to 1, so each one reads as a share of the attention.

Reach for it when a link works like a vote, as with web pages, citations between papers, or who follows whom. It needs nothing but the links.

The ideaA bored random surfer

Imagine someone browsing at random. They follow a link, and every so often they get bored and jump to any page at all. Over a long session, the share of time they spend on each page is its rank. So a link from a busy page is worth more than a link from a quiet one.

The damping factor is how often the surfer keeps clicking instead of jumping. It is usually 0.85.

Grafluent doesn’t simulate the surfer. It uses power iteration. Every page starts with an equal share. Each round, every page passes its share along its links. This repeats until no score moves by more than a small threshold you set, the tolerance. A page with no links out shares its rank with every page. The surfer on this page is there to show that the two agree.

PageRank is cheap and needs no tuning beyond the damping factor. The random jump keeps every score above 0 and makes the iteration settle.

Where it breaksPages that hoard rank

A tight cluster of pages that link only to each other can soak up rank. The random jump keeps that in check. Turn the damping all the way up to see what happens without it: here the Forum, which nobody links to, drops to nothing. On some graphs the surfer goes round a loop for ever and the scores never settle; then pageRank throws NoConvergence.

Anyone can make pages, so anyone can make links. A web search engine has to spot pages built only to raise another page’s rank.
