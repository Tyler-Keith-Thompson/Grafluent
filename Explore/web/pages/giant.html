The giant component

Say itAIR-dersh RAY-nyee/ˈɛrdøːʃ ˈreːɲi/

Add friendships at random. When does one group suddenly take in almost everyone?

Example: fifty people at a new school. Friendships form between pairs picked at random, one at a time. The connected components are the groups of people joined by chains of friends.

The ideaA tipping point at one friend each

  
In the Erdős–Rényi random graph G(n, p), each of the n(n − 1)/2 pairs is joined with probability p, independently. Its twin G(n, m) picks m edges uniformly; adding random edges one at a time, as above, passes through every G(n, m) in turn. With m edges among n people, p is about 2m/n², and the average number of friends is c = 2m/n.

  
Follow a friendship out of a group and you meet, on average, c new people, each of whom leads on to c more. When c is below 1 that chain dies out fast, so every group stays small: a few people, mostly arranged as trees. When c passes 1, which is p passing 1/n, the chain can keep going, and the many medium groups fuse into one. That giant component holds a fixed share of everyone, a share that solves s = 1 − e−cs: about 58% at c = 1.5, 80% at c = 2 and 94% at c = 3. No second group ever gets close.

  
With only fifty people the jump is gentle, and each seed tells a slightly different story. On a million people the curve stays flat near zero right up to p = 1/n, then turns sharply upward there.


  
Where it breaksReal networks aren’t coin flips

  
Erdős–Rényi graphs are the baseline, not a model of real friendships. Real networks have hubs with far more links than average, which barabasiAlbert grows by giving new links mostly to the already popular. They are also full of triangles, friends of friends being friends, which wattsStrogatz builds into a ring before rewiring a few links. Both have giant components too, but at different thresholds.

  
A giant component isn’t the whole graph, either. For everyone to be connected, isolated people included, c has to reach about ln n, which is near 3.9 for fifty people: that is why a few people are usually still alone at the end above.
