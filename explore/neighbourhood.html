Neighbourhoods

A question names Ada Lovelace. Which facts in a knowledge graph are near enough to matter, and how do you stop at a sensible number?

What it’s forThe part of a graph around a few places

A knowledge graph can hold millions of facts. A question touches a few entities, and what matters is what lies close to them: the entities a hop or two away and the facts between them. That is the neighbourhood of the question, also called its ego graph.

Reach for it to feed a language model the facts around a question, to show a person the part of a social network near them, or to cut a large graph down to a piece worth drawing. A budget keeps the piece small when a hop would bring in too much.

Hops are the simplest notion of nearness, and a budget keeps them honest. Personalised PageRank ranks by nearness instead of counting hops. A Steiner tree answers a different question: how the seeds connect.

The ideaBreadth-first from the seeds, with a cap

Start from the entities the question names. The first ring is everything one fact away from any of them. The next ring is everything one fact beyond that, and so on, up to the number of hops you allow. This is a breadth-first search from several starting points at once, and the neighbourhood is the part of the graph it reaches, with all the facts between those entities.

The budget is applied ring by ring. A ring that fits is kept whole. The first ring that would push past the budget is cut to the places left, keeping the entities of greatest priority, and the search stops there. The lab ranks by how many facts an entity has, so hubs come first; pageRank(personalization:) from the seeds is a stronger ranking for the same closure. egoGraphView(of:radius:maximumVertexCount:priority:) builds the whole view in one pass and shares the graph’s storage, so it costs nothing to make beyond the search.

Where it breaksHubs and hops

One hop from a hub is a crowd. In a graph where “England” links to a million entities, a two-hop neighbourhood of almost anything is most of the graph. The budget stops the flood but cannot say which of the million matter; that is what the priority is for, and a priority such as personalised PageRank from the seeds does better than counting links.

Hops are blind to the kind of link. A knowledge graph with typed relations, as an edge map, can filter the neighbourhood to the relations the question is about before counting hops.

In SwiftThe knowledge graph, in Grafluent

import AdjacencyListModule
import GraphOperations
import GraphProtocols

let mentions = [
    UndirectedEdge("Ada Lovelace", "Analytical Engine"), UndirectedEdge("Ada Lovelace", "Charles Babbage"),
    UndirectedEdge("Charles Babbage", "Analytical Engine"), UndirectedEdge("Charles Babbage", "Difference Engine"),
    UndirectedEdge("Ada Lovelace", "Lord Byron"), UndirectedEdge("Lord Byron", "Poetry"), UndirectedEdge("Lord Byron", "Greece"),
    UndirectedEdge("Analytical Engine", "Punched cards"), UndirectedEdge("Punched cards", "Jacquard loom"),
    UndirectedEdge("Jacquard loom", "Weaving"), UndirectedEdge("Analytical Engine", "Programming"),
    UndirectedEdge("Programming", "Alan Turing"), UndirectedEdge("Alan Turing", "Enigma"), UndirectedEdge("Alan Turing", "Bletchley Park"),
    UndirectedEdge("Difference Engine", "Logarithm tables"), UndirectedEdge("Logarithm tables", "Navigation"),
    UndirectedEdge("Charles Babbage", "Royal Society"), UndirectedEdge("Royal Society", "Isaac Newton"),
]

let facts = UndirectedAdjacencyList<String> {
    for mention in mentions { mention }
}

let seeds = ["Ada Lovelace"]
let hops = 2
let budget = 8

let nearby = facts.egoGraphView(of: seeds, radius: hops)
let entities = Array(nearby.vertices)  // ["Ada Lovelace", "Analytical Engine", "Charles Babbage", "Difference Engine", "Lord Byron", "Poetry", "Greece", "Punched cards", "Programming", "Royal Society"]
let links = nearby.edgeCount  // 10

let nearby = facts.egoGraphView(of: seeds, radius: hops, maximumVertexCount: budget) { facts.degree(of: $0) }
let entities = Array(nearby.vertices)  // ["Ada Lovelace", "Analytical Engine", "Charles Babbage", "Difference Engine", "Lord Byron", "Punched cards", "Programming", "Royal Society"]
