Explore Grafluent

Watch graph algorithms think. Every page runs the real library in your browser, one step at a time.

A graph is a way to describe anything made of connected parts. The parts, called vertices, might be cities, people, web pages or the tasks in a build. The connections, called edges, might be roads, friendships, links or dependencies between tasks. Once a problem is drawn that way, a few dozen well-studied algorithms answer most of the questions people ask about it: the quickest route, the right order, the weakest point, the groups that hang together.

Those algorithms mostly carry the names of the people who found them, and many of those names are hard to say. Each page tells you how to pronounce the name and what question the algorithm answers, then lets you watch it work, step by step, on a small example you can change. Every page runs the real Grafluent library in your browser, so what you see is exactly what your code would do.

How every page works

  • A small graph you can click and edit.
  • Step, play, go back, or drag the timeline. Each marble on the timeline is one thing the algorithm did.
  • The idea: why it works, in a paragraph.
  • Where it breaks: the input that fools it, and what to use instead.
  • In Swift: the exact code the page is running.
